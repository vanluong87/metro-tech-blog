## Why metro tunnels need a dedicated ventilation system

Unlike ordinary road tunnels, a metro tunnel is an enclosed underground space that almost always has passengers inside a train. Its ventilation system serves two distinct but closely related purposes: normal ventilation, which controls temperature, removes waste heat from trains and equipment, and maintains air quality for passengers and staff; and emergency ventilation, or smoke control, which protects human life in the event of a fire — arguably the system's single most important job, since toxic smoke spreading through an enclosed space, not direct flame, is the leading cause of death in tunnel fires.

## Normal ventilation: jet fans and ventilation shafts

Under normal operation, heat released by regenerative braking, traction motors, and wheel-rail friction tends to build up inside a tunnel, especially on lines with high train frequency. Two components handle this:

1. **Tunnel jet fans**: mounted inside the tunnel, they create an airflow along the direction of travel (combining the train's own piston effect with fan thrust), pushing warm air toward the nearest ventilation shaft to be exhausted.
2. **Vertical ventilation shafts**: vertical ducts connecting the tunnel to the surface, placed at strategic points between or near stations, allowing air exchange with the outside. The piston effect of a train moving through a tunnel — pushing air ahead of it and drawing air behind it — is also harnessed as a supplementary source of natural ventilation.

## Emergency smoke control: principles and strategies

When a fire breaks out, the ventilation system's objective changes entirely: it is no longer about cooling, but about creating a controlled airflow that keeps smoke away from passengers' escape route until everyone has cleared the danger zone. Two common strategies:

- **Longitudinal ventilation**: jet fans push smoke in a single direction along the tunnel, away from people, keeping the opposite direction (the evacuation path) smoke-free. This suits single-bore tunnels with one-directional airflow.
- **Station smoke control / smoke reservoir**: if a fire starts at a station, dedicated ceiling-mounted smoke extraction fans create a localized "smoke reservoir," holding the hot smoke layer up near the ceiling while passengers evacuate at floor level — a thermal-stratification principle that extends the time visibility and breathable air remain available near the ground.

The entire system must integrate with fire detection (smoke/heat sensors), the operations control center (OCC), and emergency procedures: once a fire alarm triggers, fans must switch modes automatically within seconds, coordinated with stopping trains, cutting traction power in the affected section, and opening emergency egress routes.

## Tokyo's approach

Tokyo's metro network, dense and running deep beneath tightly built urban areas, places heavy demands on finding workable ventilation shaft locations where surface space is scarce. Lines operating there typically combine high-capacity jet fans with a well-distributed shaft network, along with an integrated monitoring system that lets the operations control center switch quickly between normal ventilation and emergency smoke-control mode. The culture of regular emergency-response drills, common across Japan's urban rail industry, matters just as much as the hardware: even the best equipment only works if operating staff follow the right procedure in the first critical seconds of an incident.

## Takeaways

Metro tunnel ventilation design must account for two entirely different operating modes — normal and emergency — at the same time, and optimizing for one at the expense of the other is not an option. Ventilation shaft locations, fan capacity, and smoke-control strategy should be settled early, during basic design, since they directly shape the tunnel and station structure and are very costly to change once construction is underway. The long operating experience of dense networks like Tokyo's shows that investing in system integration and fast-response procedures delivers safety value at least as high as simply adding more fan capacity.
