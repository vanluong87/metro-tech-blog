## Singapore's core challenge

Singapore is a city-state with extremely limited land, so MRT underground stations are often built directly beneath major arterial roads or dense central business districts. The challenge: excavate deep stations (typically 20-40m) without permanently closing roads and without disturbing adjacent high-rise buildings.

## The dominant construction method

**Diaphragm walls combined with top-down construction** is the go-to solution:

- Reinforced concrete diaphragm walls are built down to bedrock or a competent bearing layer, forming a retaining and water-cutoff system around the excavation.
- The topmost slab is cast first (top-down), then soil beneath it is excavated while lower slabs are cast in sequence from top to bottom.
- The key benefit: the road surface above can be restored and reopened to traffic soon after the top slab is cast, while excavation and structural work below continue underneath.

## Managing schedule and risk

- **Phasing construction in short sections** along the road corridor reduces the footprint occupied at any given time.
- **Real-time monitoring of diaphragm wall deflection and settlement of nearby structures**, with early-warning thresholds to halt work when needed.
- **Close coordination with urban traffic authorities** to plan traffic diversions and schedule surface-impacting work at night.

## Lessons for dense cities

Singapore's "top-down to restore the road surface early" approach is a highly practical lesson for cities with heavy traffic, where multi-year road closures are simply not socially or economically feasible.
