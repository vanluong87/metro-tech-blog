## Why Bangkok builds up, not down

Bangkok sits on soft, weak clay with a high water table, which makes tunneling significantly more expensive and technically risky than in many other cities. As a result, most of its urban rail network — notably the BTS Skytrain and large sections of the MRT lines — is built elevated, running on viaducts along the median or corridor of major roads. This approach is common across Southeast Asian cities facing similar geotechnical and budget constraints.

## The basic construction sequence

Elevated metro construction typically repeats a standard sequence along the alignment:

1. **Pile and pier construction:** Bored piles are driven down to a competent bearing layer, then a pile cap and reinforced concrete pier shaft are cast. Pier spacing (span length) is usually 25-35m depending on loading and the traffic conditions below.
2. **Viaduct girder erection:** Two main methods are used — precast segmental girders lifted into place with a launching gantry running along the piers, or cast-in-place girders using falsework. The precast-plus-gantry method is preferred in dense cities because it minimizes the time roads below are obstructed.
3. **Track, overhead/third rail systems, and safety barriers:** Once the girder structure is complete, track and traction power equipment are installed on top of the deck.
4. **Elevated station construction:** Stations are usually built on widened pier sections at intersections or transfer points, with stairs and elevators connecting down to street level.

## Advantages of going elevated

- **Significantly lower construction cost** than underground alignments, especially where soft ground would otherwise require expensive ground treatment.
- **Faster construction**, particularly with precast girders and launching gantries, which minimizes excavation and disruption to existing underground utilities.
- **Easier structural inspection and maintenance** compared with tunnels, since the structure is directly accessible from outside.

## Challenges and how Bangkok addresses them

- **Noise and vibration** affecting nearby buildings — mitigated with rubber pads between rail and girder, and noise barriers along residential sections.
- **Urban streetscape and the space beneath the viaduct:** Many sections in Bangkok repurpose the space under the elevated structure for parking, walkways, or green areas rather than leaving it unused.
- **Construction amid heavy traffic:** Erecting girders with a gantry that travels along already-built piers limits the footprint needed on the road below, and such work is often scheduled at night.

## Lessons for other cities

Bangkok's experience shows that elevated construction is often the more economical choice for suburban corridors or areas with weak soil, while sections through historic centers or areas with strict streetscape requirements may still warrant tunneling. Several other Southeast Asian cities have drawn on this hybrid approach when planning their own urban rail networks.
