## What is a TBM?

A Tunnel Boring Machine (TBM) is a mechanized excavation system that cuts through soil and rock at the tunnel face while simultaneously installing the precast concrete lining segments right behind the cutter head. Compared to traditional methods (drill-and-blast, NATM), TBM offers steadier progress, less disruption to the urban surface, and greater safety in soft ground with high water tables.

## Why Japan leads in TBM technology

Japan is one of the earliest and most extensive adopters of TBM for metro construction, for three main reasons:

1. **Complex geology**: large urban areas such as Tokyo and Osaka sit on soft alluvial deposits interspersed with groundwater pockets, requiring shields that can actively balance face pressure — either Earth Pressure Balance (EPB) or Slurry Shield machines.
2. **High urban density**: underground works must pass beneath homes, existing structures, and other infrastructure without causing significant surface settlement.
3. **Seismic resilience requirements**: segmental linings must be designed to withstand deformation from earthquakes, so materials and joints between segments are carefully engineered to remain watertight while retaining some ductility.

## Lessons worth applying elsewhere

- **Detailed geotechnical surveys before selecting the shield type**: EPB machines suit clay and sandy-clay soils, while Slurry Shields suit loose sandy soils with high groundwater.
- **Continuous surface settlement monitoring** throughout construction, allowing timely adjustment of face pressure to protect adjacent structures.
- **Segmental lining design that accounts for seismic loads**, not just static loads.

For cities with soft soil and high building density — such as Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi — Japan's experience in managing settlement risk and matching shield type to local geology is a highly valuable reference.
