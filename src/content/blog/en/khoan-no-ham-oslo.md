## What is drill and blast?

Drill and blast is a traditional tunneling method suited to hard, relatively unfractured bedrock — ground conditions where a TBM or shotcrete-based NATM may not be the most economical choice. The basic cycle repeats in short rounds: drilling blast holes at the tunnel face according to a pre-calculated pattern, charging and detonating them in a timed sequence (delays) so the rock breaks in the intended direction, ventilating and mucking out the broken rock, and then installing temporary ground support (rock bolts, mesh, shotcrete) before starting the next round.

## Why Norway is closely associated with drill and blast

Norway has a long tradition of tunneling through bedrock — mostly gneiss and granite — stemming from the need to build roads, railways, and hydropower tunnels through steep fjord terrain. As Oslo developed its T-bane system (a combined underground/surface metro), many sections pass through shallow, competent rock beneath only a thin soil cover, which suits drill and blast far better than more expensive mechanized methods. Three factors keep the method in continued use:

1. **Stable bedrock geology**: hard, relatively unfractured rock allows large tunnel cross-sections to be excavated without heavy support, unlike the soft urban soils common in many Asian cities.
2. **Lower equipment investment than TBM**: drill and blast relies on self-propelled drilling rigs (jumbo drills) and conventional mucking equipment, making it well suited to short tunnels, irregular cross-sections, or complex station branches.
3. **Decades of experience managing urban blasting**: Norway's construction industry has developed rigorous procedures for controlling vibration, noise, and post-blast fumes (ventilation) to work safely near residential areas.

## Managing risk in urban drill and blast work

- **Blast design** — the pattern of holes and detonation sequence — controls the direction the rock breaks and limits vibration transmitted to nearby structures, typically using delayed detonation across groups of holes.
- **Vibration monitoring (seismographs)** at sensitive nearby structures, with alert thresholds used to adjust the explosive charge for the next round.
- **Forced ventilation after every blast** to clear toxic gases (CO, NOx) before workers return to the face, paired with automated gas monitoring.
- **Prompt temporary support after mucking**: rock bolts and mesh are installed quickly to stabilize the tunnel arch before the next drilling round, minimizing the time the face is left unsupported.

## Lessons worth applying elsewhere

Norway's experience shows that drill and blast is not an "outdated" method — it remains an economical and effective choice wherever the geology suits it, particularly for short tunnels, geometrically complex underground stations, or tunnels through shallow bedrock. For cities with mixed hard rock and soft soil conditions, combining drill and blast for rock sections with TBM or NATM for soft-ground sections — together with strict vibration and fume control procedures — is a strategy worth considering to optimize cost while keeping the surrounding urban area safe.
