## What is jet grouting?

Jet grouting is a ground improvement technique in which a small drill rod is advanced to a target depth and then, as it is withdrawn, high-pressure nozzles inject cement grout (typically a cement-water-additive mixture) that erodes the surrounding soil and mixes it in place with the grout. Once the grout sets, the resulting soil-cement mass forms columns with significantly improved strength and reduced permeability compared to the natural ground — without the need to excavate and replace soil, as traditional improvement methods often require.

## Why Hanoi needs this technique

Much of central Hanoi sits on soft ground typical of the Red River delta: alternating layers of soft clay, silty clay, and saturated fine sand with low bearing capacity and a tendency to deform under construction loads or deep excavation. When building metro tunnels and underground stations — particularly at station ends, shaft locations, or where a TBM launches or is received — this soft ground creates several risks:

1. **Excavation wall instability** when groundwater is drawn down or deep cuts are made without adequate ground support.
2. **Uneven settlement of nearby structures** caused by stress changes in soft soil during construction.
3. **Difficulty controlling groundwater seepage** through fine sand layers, which can lead to piping or base heave in excavations.

Jet grouting is favored because it can be executed flexibly within the tight spatial constraints of a dense city, can create improved soil masses of precise shape and location (single columns, strips, or continuous blocks), and performs well across a range of soil types — from soft clay to fine sand — the mixed geology commonly found along Hanoi's metro corridors.

## Basic construction sequence

- **Guided drilling**: the drill rod with a jetting monitor is advanced to the design depth at a pre-surveyed location and inclination.
- **High-pressure jet mixing**: as the rod rotates and is withdrawn at a controlled rate, cement grout is injected at very high pressure (often hundreds of bar), shredding and mixing the in-situ soil with the grout.
- **Real-time monitoring of construction parameters**: injection pressure, withdrawal rate, and grout flow are continuously logged to ensure the resulting column diameter and strength meet the design requirements.
- **Verification coring** after the grout has cured, to confirm the actual compressive strength and column diameter against the design values.

## Role within the overall metro project

Jet grouting ground improvement is typically carried out before a TBM launches or before deep station excavations begin, creating a temporary "improved ground block" with enough strength and water-tightness for the following excavation work to proceed safely. It is an important preparatory step that often draws less attention than headline activities like tunnel boring or station structural works — yet poor quality control in ground improvement can lead to serious problems in later construction stages. For metro projects in soft-ground cities like Hanoi, detailed geotechnical investigation, improvement designs tailored to each soil type, and close monitoring of jet grouting parameters are key to keeping subsequent underground works on schedule and safe.
